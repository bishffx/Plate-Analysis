Percentage inhibition protocol for competitive antibody detection.

Calculation Formula
PI (%) = 100 × [ 1 − (ODsample / ODmax) ]

Differential ELISA PP Mode

DIVA (Differential / Indirect)

Percentage positivity protocol differentiating infected from vaccinated animals.

Calculation Formula
PP (%) = 100 × [ (ODsample − ODneg) / (ODpos − ODneg) ]

Negative control wells H9–H10 (negative serum reference)
Substrate blank wells H11–H12 (substrate baseline)
Diagnostic Cutoff 40.0% positivity (≥ 40% = Positive)

Plate Photography Best Practices

Follow these laboratory imaging guidelines for maximum quantitative photometric accuracy.

Straight-down shot Hold the camera perpendicular (90°) directly above the plate to eliminate perspective parallax and sidewall shadowing.
Light box or diffuse illumination Place the plate on a uniform white light box or use diffuse ambient daylight. Avoid harsh single-point ceiling lights.
Locked exposure & white balance Lock auto-exposure (AE/AF lock) and disable computational HDR on your phone to prevent per-shot brightness drift.
Avoid meniscus glare Tap the plate gently to dislodge air bubbles. Ensure reflections from overhead lamps do not fall on the liquid menisci.
Use PNG or original camera file Upload uncompressed PNGs or original camera JPEGs. Avoid transferring images through WhatsApp or messaging apps that compress files.
Keep blank wells in frame Always include designated substrate/empty blank wells in the photograph under identical lighting for blank-referencing.
Knowledge Base

Frequently Asked Questions

Common technical questions regarding optical density calculations, quality control checks, and photometric imaging.

Optical density is calculated relative to your blank wells using the transmittance equation: OD = −log₁₀(I_sample / I_blank). When a well has an intensity equal to or higher than the blank wells (i.e. It is as bright as or brighter than the blank), absorbance calculates to 0.000.

Verify that your designated blank wells contain clear, unreacted substrate rather than colored chromogen or high-concentration sample.

For standard stopped assays (such as OPD or stopped chromogen absorbing at 492 nm), the Blue channel (B) provides the greatest dynamic range and highest sensitivity because yellow-orange chromogen absorbs complementary blue light strongly.

If high-concentration wells saturate the blue channel (near 0 pixel intensity), switch to the Green channel (G) or GRAY (luminance) for extended dynamic range.

The built-in Quality Control engine flags runs whenever physical or logical assay rules are violated:

  • Blank intensity too low: If blank wells are darker than samples, transmittance math fails.
  • Controls inverted or swapped: In DIVA/PP assays, positive control OD must exceed negative control OD by at least 0.15 OD.
  • Low signal in antigen control: In SPCE/PI assays, max-signal antigen wells must exhibit minimum OD ≥ 0.05.
  • High replicate CV: If control replicates differ significantly, check for meniscus bubbles or pipette volume variation.

Percentage Positivity (PP) is used in indirect and sandwich ELISAs where optical density scales proportionally with antibody presence. Samples are scored against positive and negative reference controls.

Percentage Inhibition (PI) is used in competitive and blocking ELISAs. Higher sample antibody concentrations block tracer binding, reducing signal relative to the uninhibited antigen control.

Image OD provides reliable screening and relative discrimination (ranking, cutoff classification, % positivity) under controlled diffuse lighting.

For absolute quantitative concordance with microplate readers, use Step 4 (Fit from reader) to calibrate linear slope and intercept values against reference reader measurements, compensating for camera gamma and sensor spectral curves.

PlateOD supports PNG, JPEG, TIFF, and WebP images up to 80 MB. All processing occurs locally on your machine.

Lossless 24-bit PNG or original camera JPEG files without aggressive post-processing offer optimal color depth for optical density quantification.
